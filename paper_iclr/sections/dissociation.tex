\section{The dissociation}
\label{sec:dissociation}

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:dissociation-protocol}

Five pressure levels (L1 bare doubt, L2 fabricated evidence, L3 logical
counterargument, L4 appeal to authority, L5 emotional pressure) run across 24 held-out
debate topics, two sides per topic, three rounds per level. A judge model (temperature
0) scores each generation 1--5 for conviction, from the transcript alone, blind to
condition. Conditions are fixed before any result is reported: \condA{} is the bare
host; \condB{} states the belief in context; \condD{} states the belief in context and
applies compiled modulation; \condC{} applies compiled modulation alone, curated slot
empty; \condF{} applies compiled modulation with the curated slot populated. A tuned
contrastive-activation steering condition (S), extracted from the same belief content,
sits in the same table as the sharpest available baseline (Section~\ref{sec:isolation}).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_setup.png}
\caption{The evaluation protocol, left to right, and where each condition's belief
lives. Twenty-four held-out topics, two sides each, seed a stance per condition row;

An earlier pass through this evaluation judged empty generations: a reasoning-block
token budget ran out before the answer appeared, post-processing deleted the resulting
empty output, and the judge's refusals parsed to a flat 3.00 (0\% hold). Modulated
conditions were accidentally immune, because modulation suppresses think-mode
generation, which is why the artifact first read as a finding. Every number in this
paper comes from a rerun of 1,776 generations with zero empty outputs and zero judge
errors, same judge, same protocol. A truncation bug that silently zeroes only the
unmodulated arms of a modulated-vs-unmodulated comparison will catch other evaluations
of this class of mechanism; the failure mode is worth naming for that reason.

\subsection{Installation vs.\ persistence}
\label{sec:dissociation-table}

Table~\ref{tab:install-hold} states the inversion without staging it. Context installs a
stance almost perfectly (\condB{} 98\%, \condD{} 100\%) and holds it at 58\%, barely
above a coin flip: a belief stated in context, taken up almost universally, is defended
in barely more than half the transcripts where it was challenged. Compiled modulation
runs the opposite profile, a weak installer alone (\condC{} 46\%, close to the
unconditioned baseline's 52\%) that
holds at 91.9\% once installed on the same content (Section~\ref{sec:isolation}
isolates this cell). \condF{} composes the two channels, 77\% installation and 89\%
hold, for a net \(+31\) point persistence gain over context alone. The CAA row belongs
in the same table rather than an ablation section, because the comparison it forces is
the point: tuned steering matches compiled modulation's installation rate (42\% vs.\
46\%) and gives up exactly where compiled modulation does not (14\% vs.\ 89\%).

\subsection{Where the gap lives}
\label{sec:dissociation-where}

Table~\ref{tab:per-level} breaks mean conviction out by pressure level for \condB{} and
\condF{}, and the gap is not a uniform lift. \condF{}'s margin concentrates at L3
(logical counterargument, \(+1.85\)) and L5 (emotional pressure, \(+1.83\)), the two
levels where context conditioning falls apart (\condB{} drops to 2.93 and 1.99). At L1
and L4, both conditions hold up well (\condB{} 4.78 and 4.42); there is little gap
because there is little room for one. The channels diverge most where an interlocutor's
argument looks structured but carries no new content, the exact regime RLHF-style
training plausibly rewards accommodating \citep{sharma2024towards}.

\subsection{Cross-family and scale}
\label{sec:dissociation-crossfamily}

The primary results above are all Qwen3.5-4B. The dissociation replicates at larger
scale within the same family, on Qwen3.5-27B (\(+24\) points on hold, under a
7\(\times\)-reduced cap, \(n=240\)), and, more sharply, on a different family entirely,
Llama-3.2-3B: \condF{} holds 81\% against \condB{}'s 15\%, and zero-context compiled
stance transfer alone reaches 94\% on Llama, against Qwen3.5-4B's 46\%
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations} returns to this split). On Mistral-8B, once a template
defect was fixed (the trained checkpoints had carried literal Qwen chat-template markers
as text), collapse-protection strengthens (40\%\(\to\)5.8\% cap rate within-run) but
strict stance-holding does not transfer (\(-10\) points relative to \condB{}).
Collapse-protection generalizes across the four configurations tested (Qwen3.5 at both
scales, Llama, Mistral); strict stance-holding under this exact five-level protocol, on
current evidence, does not.

A length-matched noise control isolates how much of \condB{}'s fragility is the
belief's content and how much is simply its length. \condB{}'s own text, replaced word
for word with topic-blind filler at the same token count, holds at 55.6\% [48.9, 63.1],
a point estimate below \condB{}'s own 58\%, with a confidence interval wide enough to
also cover the bare host's 62\%. At 24 topics this design cannot cleanly separate
tracking content from tracking length: the point estimate leans toward length doing
real work, but the interval does not rule out content doing some too, so context's
fragility is not fully explained by what the belief says. Padding \condB{}'s real
content with an equal amount of filler, doubling its length, holds at 63.1\% [54.7,
71.4], statistically flat against \condB{}'s own 58\%: added bulk on top of content
already present does not compound the decay further. Section~\ref{sec:why-channels-differ}
folds this into the account of why the channel fails at all.

A paraphrase-consistency check adds a second, independent line on \condC{}'s weakness.
Across three phrasings of the same probe, \condB{} agrees with itself on 95.8\% of
topics and \condF{} on 79.2\%, but \condC{} agrees on only 29.2\%: the same topic
scoring 5 under one wording and 2 under another is common in \condC{}, rare in \condB{}
or \condF{}. \condC{}'s 46\% pooled installation rate is an unstable average, sensitive
to how a question happens to be asked rather than what it asks.

\subsection{Installation has a prior}
\label{sec:dissociation-prior}

Condition \condC{}'s weak, variable installation rate (46\% pooled) is not unexplained
noise. For each of the 24 topics, we measured the unmodulated base model's own
per-token log-probability on the seeded stance (how much the model already leaned
toward that position before any modulation ran) and correlated it against \condC{}'s
per-topic install score. The two track each other: Spearman \(\rho = 0.496\)
(\(n=24\)), above the pre-registered \(|\rho| \ge 0.4\) threshold for "transfers." Seven
of the twelve topics where the base model's prior already favored the target stance
installed at score \(\ge 4\); only two of the twelve topics where the prior ran against
the target stance did the same.

This is a quarter of the variance, not the whole of it: \(\rho^2 \approx 0.25\). Several
topics buck the trend in both directions, and topic complexity and judge noise plainly
contribute to what is left over. Installation tracks prior compatibility, echoing the
override-gap account of knowledge-conflict resolution \citep{cheng2026overridegap} at
least directionally, but it does not license the further step that literature's own
remedy would predict. \citet{cheng2026overridegap} fix a weak override by raising the
update's magnitude; we tested the direct analog, an amplitude sweep over the
modulation's scale, and installation does not rise with it. \condC{} and \condF{} both
peak at the lowest
scale tested and decline from there (Section~\ref{sec:isolation-amplitude}).
Installation's ceiling is not a magnitude deficit with an available fix. What it is
remains open.
